# Narrated

A concise documentary essay with an openly new narrator, supported by authentic archive pictures and brief original-sound passages. Appropriate for archives with visual coverage and reliable source records but needing connective context. The narrator explains relationships, not merely describes what is already visible.

## Script and picture
Log the archive before drafting. Pick one factual story, make a claim/source/proving-shot table, then write a short script with setup, a meaningful transition and a supported resolution. Delete claims that have no reliable evidence or picture. Do not invent private thoughts, danger, causality or dialogue. Say when a shot or quotation comes from a different time. Read the script naturally; picture sequences should follow its ideas and develop scale, human activity and results rather than one random shot per sentence.

## New voice and authentic sound
Use letitedit_voice's default voice, with no reference clip and no clone of a mission participant. Identify the result as newly generated narration in credits/evidence, never historical audio. Settle the voice timing before the narration-led rough picture, leave meaningful breaths, and place a small number of real archive audio extracts with clear context. Score around the narration and the authentic extracts; avoid wall-to-wall speech. Exact accessible captions may accompany narration. A finished web mix starts near -14 LUFS, <= -1 dBTP, checked for every important word.

## Distinct from Archive story
This Look's new script is the spine; Archive story's original voices are its spine. Do not simply lay a generic voice over an existing cut or repeat its picture order. Verify dates and factual claims against primary records. A supplied folder is appropriate only if the pictures can prove the new script; otherwise prepare another practical folder and explain why.

## Working from footage
Log the whole supplied folder by picture, and sound where present, before selecting. Neutral filenames are not descriptions. Build a shot ledger with original path and span, the shot's purpose, and what changes between shots. Wide, detail, human reaction and result are different information, not interchangeable decorations. Select from alternatives; do not cover missing material with fake camera angles or stock. Plan beginning, development and ending explicitly. Read the relevant craft/modes, story, rough, fine, sound, finish and gate skills in full at their stages; this is not Turbo.

For phone delivery, prefer subject-aware 9:16 crops where honest; retain an archive image whole on a designed background when a crop loses essential evidence. Never stretch or leave the important action tiny. Titles, picture and access captions share one safe layout, away from faces and platform controls. Check actual export frames at phone size and the mix, not only the timeline.

Check that the archive picture itself remains large enough in the portrait composition during the rough cut. Inspect people, action, detail and results at their least forgiving moments; a correct aspect ratio with a tiny image or a large empty lower frame still fails. Correct the layout before the full review, without cropping away evidence needed by the script.

## Must not
No invented claims, chronology, dialogue, endorsement or product brand. No silent export, endless generic music bed, obligatory transitions, punch-ins masquerading as shot selection, unexplained source watermarks, or cloning an archive speaker. Do not apply a rigid cut rate to every story. Let authentic sound and pictures keep their meaning; if sound comes from another time or source identify it rather than passing it off as synchronous.

## Evidence and limits
- [Educational / medium] Yale Film Analysis, Editing: spatial relations, continuity, montage and the selection/order of shots construct meaning. It does not establish a universally good cutting speed. https://filmanalysis.yale.edu/editing/
- [Practitioner / medium] Brendan Bellomo on Porcelain War, Art of the Cut: picture, music and emotional structure are developed together, with room for contrast. A practitioner account, not a controlled retention experiment. https://borisfx.com/blog/aotc/art-of-the-cut-porcelain-war/
- [Primary / strong for source permissions only] NASA Images and Media Usage Guidelines: credit NASA; inspect each source for third-party content; do not imply endorsement; likeness/marks can need separate clearance. https://www.nasa.gov/nasa-brand-center/images-and-media/
- Durations, typography, density, music character and amount of treatment are creative starting points [low], adapted to the actual brief. No numerical retention promise.

## Source and delivery limits
Read source and licence records and preserve source credits beside the export. Government-media status does not clear likenesses, insignia, contractor marks or voices for promotion and does not imply endorsement. Choose genuinely distinct clips rather than repeated crops of one, and keep a source-selection record. Check the complete moving export, sound, poster and one-frame-per-second contact sheets; an attractive image or automated verdict alone is insufficient.
