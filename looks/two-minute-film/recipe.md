# Two-minute film

A complete short narrative, not a trailer with a longer montage.

## Selection and structure

Find a story the footage actually supports. Establish the principal people and
their situation, a goal or tension, consequential attempts and a final changed
state. Log the whole film or shoot before selecting. Condensation works by
leaving out repetition while preserving the causes that make the remaining
actions intelligible. Protect setups needed for later payoffs. Reorder only when
it does not falsely change chronology or character motivation. A brief may ask
for about two minutes; a slightly shorter complete scene is better than padding,
and a required runtime still needs checking before delivery.

Choose action windows, not just scenes. An approaching threat may need a wide
view for geography, but entering that shot too early leaves the audience waiting
without a changing situation. Check the complete held range in motion: preserve
the approach, passage, reaction and reversal that earn the payoff, while leaving
out empty anticipation. If the story needs more time, earn it through meaningful
character choices or construction consequences, not a longer wait before a gag.

## Performance, rhythm and sound

Give each consequential look or physical action time to register. Use wider
views when multiple performers or objects explain the action, with details
for readable choices and consequences. Keep screen direction, geography, object
identity and eyelines coherent. Scene-to-scene pace can rise and fall rather
than accelerating constantly. Music is a score to this complete arc, not a bed
faded out at an arbitrary source second. Dialogue and production sound remain
primary when available. Silent-film footage can use cleared instrumental music;
do not fabricate speech or claim invented effects are original. Words should
bridge only what the footage cannot explain without changing the story.
Portrait is preferred for a phone-oriented piece only if it preserves the
performance and cause; otherwise explain the intentional landscape composition.

## Evidence and limits

- [Practitioner synthesis] StudioBinder's Soviet Montage Theory,
  https://www.studiobinder.com/blog/soviet-montage-theory/, describes contrasting
  and supporting images, pace and tone. It does not establish that montage alone tells
  a complete narrative or validates this particular recipe.
- [Practitioner references] The performance and comedy discussions in
  skills/letitedit-modes/references/creative-stylised.md support story clarity
  over devices and protected setup-to-punchline timing, not fixed shot lengths.
- [Proposed heuristics] Cause, attempt and consequence, preserved gag setup,
  and a genuine ending are local editorial tests. Ask a critic to retell the
  rendered story, and independently compare it with the source. Neither a
  loudness reading nor a clean montage is proof of narrative coherence.
- Source-window selection needs a motion check as well as a story retell; fixed seconds per shot cannot settle it.

## Source and delivery limits

Tell a complete condensation from distinct source ranges, not a recycled trailer. Preserve the source aspect ratio without stretching, and intentional landscape where simultaneous action and geography matter. Exclude later scores unless recording rights are verified.
