# Hands at work

A tactile, visually driven account of material changing under real hands.
It observes a transformation rather than turning it into a numbered lesson.
The footage sets the rhythm and visual treatment; this is not a template.

## Purpose and driver

Choose a material change the supplied footage can carry honestly. The initial
state, consequential actions and visible stopping point form the spine. Follow
cause and effect, without inventing a problem, a setback or a triumphant finish.
A quiet practical achievement can be enough. A selected film theme may guide
light, framing or pace, but must preserve the evidence and the material's colour.

## Picture choices

Keep object identity across the sequence. Log changes of object or camera setup
before treating two shots as before and after. Prefer actions with readable
approach, contact and consequence; a close view earns its place by revealing
pressure, texture or shape. A wider view can reorient the viewer. Cut repeated
work when it adds no visible change, but retain the movement that explains a
result. A sped-up passage must not imply impossible speed or ease.

In portrait framing, keep hands, tool and affected material legible together.
An intentional wide composition is valid when a crop would lose the action.
Let motion, pauses, and changing shape guide joins. Do not cut merely to hit a
numerical pace target. Give the actual final state a moment to register.

## Words and sound

Sparse, truthful context or the maker's actual voice can accompany the work.
There is no requirement for step numbers, a presenter, a voiceover or large
headline cards. Caption only real speech. Cleared work sounds can lead; music
can provide phrases around meaningful changes. Preserve informative natural
sound when rights permit it. Silence is a legitimate alternative, not evidence
of ASMR quality or a sound-led edit. Never manufacture source sound or record
the person's microphone.

## Must not

Do not splice different objects into a false transformation. Do not invent
adversity, time saved, skill, safety, a finished product or a before shot.
Wet clay is not fired, glazed, food-safe or ready to use. Do not make a stronger
ending than the source provides. Do not replace the work with unrelated graphics.

## Source and delivery limits

Keep object identity and material stages honest: wet shaped clay is not a finished pot. Verify rights for original music and any replacement; an available stream or private review is not clearance. Preserve source credits and report missing usable sound.
