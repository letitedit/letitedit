# Satisfying process

Real material, hands, tool contact and the sound of work carry the edit.

## Footage fit

Use a coherent hands-on project with close work, visible physical changes, useful
coverage and its actual work sounds. Log the folder for approach, contact,
material response and a real stopping point. A distant industrial assembly or
a silent stock clip does not prove this Look. If the folder cannot support the
sound-led promise, identify the missing coverage rather than fake ASMR.

## Selection, timing and design

Begin with a tactile action or the material before contact; orient when needed.
Group actions into readable progress, vary shot scale and action length, then
give the achieved state space to register. Hold through a consequential contact
instead of severing it to meet a cut-rate target. Compress repeated labor without
implying that a different object is the result. Keep hand, tool and affected
material together in portrait. Few words are usually enough; a label is not a
substitute for visible cause and effect. No compulsory narration or step numbers.

In a phone frame, the working contact is the subject, not the worker's head.
Prefer genuine close coverage and shot-specific crops that fill the picture
while retaining the approaching tool, contact and changing surface. Check the
whole action range, not just the crop's best frame. A thin sharp landscape
strip surrounded by blurred duplicates is not a successful portrait reframe.
Use a wider view briefly for orientation; if important simultaneous action
cannot share a portrait crop, explain the need for landscape rather than
hiding the loss with a dominant blurred background.

## Sound

Audition the original files. Choose cuts around actual attacks, texture,
release and decay. Room tone can join camera changes; music is optional and
must leave the work intelligible. Match discontinuous levels without flattening
every contact. Do not generate replacement work sounds, imply synchronization
where it does not exist, record a microphone, or advertise silence as tactile
sound design. Resolve the last action and retain a little real decay.

## Evidence and limits

- [Practitioner references] Art of the Cut: Porcelain War,
  https://borisfx.com/blog/aotc/art-of-the-cut-porcelain-war/, and the sources in
  skills/letitedit-modes/references/montage-transformation.md discuss subject-led
  selections and purposeful rhythm. This is an analogy to craft coverage, not
  an experiment establishing ASMR or retention effects.
- [Proposed heuristics] Contact-led joins, varying texture and a held final state
  are material-dependent choices. There is no universal pace or sound level for
  satisfaction. Check actual sound and motion rather than judging still frames.

## Source and delivery limits

Use genuine hands-on process coverage with cleared work sound. An already-edited source or registration status does not establish complete raw coverage, audio rights or quality.
