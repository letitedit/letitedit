# See it work

A function-first demonstration: object, real use, observable result. The
picture earns each statement. This is an editing recipe, not a sales template.

## Purpose and driver

Establish which object or function is being demonstrated and what the supplied
coverage can prove. Let the actual use lead. The viewer should connect the
object, the action and its visible consequence without relying on a slogan.
Branding, a presenter, a CTA, music and a hero shot are options when supplied
and relevant, not compulsory ingredients.

## Claim-to-shot choices

Keep a small claim-to-shot ledger in the story plan. For each factual statement,
record the source moment or supplied verified fact that supports it, together
with any limit. Prefer readable operation and a result over multiple beauty
shots. Keep enough context to identify what is doing the work. Select detail
views that reveal the interaction rather than hiding constraints.

Use real chronological operation when causality matters. A comparison requires
comparable coverage and conditions; a different object or shot is not evidence
of improvement. Reframe around the product and its contact with the work. Where
portrait cropping hides either, use a deliberate wider composition or inset.
Do not imply that tightly cropped hands alone show the whole product.

## Words and sound

Use only verified names, specs, limitations and claims. If there is no narration,
short observational text can clarify the function; do not label it as a spoken
caption. Synchronize each line with its proving action. Cleared real operation
sound, relevant speech, quiet music or silence can serve the demonstration.
Sound effects must not fabricate performance or make a silent mechanism sound
powerful. Never record the microphone.

## Must not

Do not invent a brand, price, recommendation, endorsement, spec, CTA or test.
Visible operation does not prove that a product is faster, precise, easy, safe,
durable or better. Do not conceal an omitted step with a claim of instant results.
Do not call wet clay a usable finished product. No manufactured comparison.

## Source and delivery limits

An operation must be legible at phone size, and any qualification must be readable. Do not call an unfinished material a usable product or mix different objects into one demonstration. Already edited footage supports a limited use-study, not proof of whole-product coverage. Mute uncleared music and disclose missing evidence; preserve required credits, source, licence and change notices. Public use needs any applicable people and marketing clearances.
