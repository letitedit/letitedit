# Archive story

A factual short documentary carried by the archive's own pictures, mission voices and carefully chosen dates. Suitable for an archive with several phases of one event and usable original audio; not a history slideshow or a string of unrelated famous moments.

## Evidence-led story
Choose one historical question or change the footage can actually demonstrate. Establish who/where/when, develop a challenge or transition, then land the documented result and its consequence. Log source speech with times and distinguish recorded announcements, mission communications and later commentary. Pick complete truthful extracts; retain qualifiers and do not join words into a new claim. Not every picture needs speech, and not every source voice belongs in the piece. Chronological ordering is one option, but a factual flash-forward must be signalled.

Use dates/locations at genuine time jumps, restrained identifying titles and occasional short context statements. Facts come from the source records and primary mission history, not an inferred image. Let the actual archive voices carry the important beats with exact calm access captions. Music supports rather than retells the mission, steps down under voices, and resolves after the final recorded thought. Preserve useful radio texture without forcing modern polish; don't fabricate radio chatter, Foley or astronaut voices. A quiet musical/room-tone bridge is preferable to dead digital silence when pictures change.

## Archive coverage
Choose source material that covers preparation, action and result. Choose a narrower defensible story rather than cram every fact into a minute. Mission audio must be located and heard; an audio stream is not evidence that the chosen spans contain relevant voices. If the original sound is unusable, report the lack instead of silently converting this Look into Narrated.

Verify actual audio before treating a transcript as dialogue: speech recognition can hallucinate words over silence. Inspect audio files as well as videos. Separately sourced original archive audio needs source/time records and an archive-extract label where synchronism is unestablished. Never attach it to arbitrary pictures as if they were synchronized.

## Working from footage
Log the whole supplied folder by picture, and sound where present, before selecting. Neutral filenames are not descriptions. Build a shot ledger with original path and span, the shot's purpose, and what changes between shots. Wide, detail, human reaction and result are different information, not interchangeable decorations. Select from alternatives; do not cover missing material with fake camera angles or stock. Plan beginning, development and ending explicitly. Read the relevant craft/modes, story, rough, fine, sound, finish and gate skills in full at their stages; this is not Turbo.

For phone delivery, prefer subject-aware 9:16 crops where honest; retain an archive image whole on a designed background when a crop loses essential evidence. Never stretch or leave the important action tiny. Titles, picture and access captions share one safe layout, away from faces and platform controls. Check actual export frames at phone size and the mix, not only the timeline.

## Must not
No invented claims, chronology, dialogue, endorsement or product brand. No silent export, endless generic music bed, obligatory transitions, punch-ins masquerading as shot selection, unexplained source watermarks, or cloning an archive speaker. Do not apply a rigid cut rate to every story. Let authentic sound and pictures keep their meaning; if sound comes from another time or source identify it rather than passing it off as synchronous.

## Evidence and limits
- [Educational / medium] Yale Film Analysis, Editing: spatial relations, continuity, montage and the selection/order of shots construct meaning. It does not establish a universally good cutting speed. https://filmanalysis.yale.edu/editing/
- [Practitioner / medium] Brendan Bellomo on Porcelain War, Art of the Cut: picture, music and emotional structure are developed together, with room for contrast. A practitioner account, not a controlled retention experiment. https://borisfx.com/blog/aotc/art-of-the-cut-porcelain-war/
- [Primary / strong for source permissions only] NASA Images and Media Usage Guidelines: credit NASA; inspect each source for third-party content; do not imply endorsement; likeness/marks can need separate clearance. https://www.nasa.gov/nasa-brand-center/images-and-media/
- Durations, typography, density, music character and amount of treatment are creative starting points [low], adapted to the actual brief. No numerical retention promise.

## Source and delivery limits
Read source and licence records and preserve credits beside the export. Public-domain government media does not clear likenesses, insignia, contractor marks or voices for promotion, or imply endorsement. Select genuinely distinct clips rather than repeated crops of one; keep a source-selection record. Check the complete moving export and sound, not just its poster or automated verdict.
