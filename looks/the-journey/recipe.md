# The journey

Travel film: arrive, discover, leave with a changed sense of the place. Pictures and location sound lead; music gives the visit shape rather than hiding every sound.

## Structure and selection

Read the raw folder, provenance and available sound first. Find actual arrival or orientation material (road, transport, map, first view), exploration (people doing something, paths, details and landmarks), and a release (leaving, a retreating view or a last quiet observation). An impression of a visit is valid; never claim a documented single day or traveler when the collection spans seasons or people. Do not invent obstacles, dialogue or reactions. Name the beginning, middle and end before building.

Select across the folder in your own order, not the source reel's order. Match wide geography with medium human action and close textures. Build small related sequences; a list of unrelated scenic wides is not a journey. Repeated views compete for a place, not automatic inclusion. Give an important discovery room before moving on. A gentle passage and an energetic passage can coexist. No mandatory transition pack, grade or exact cut interval.

## Sound, picture and finish

Audition the originals. Let identifiable footsteps, water, transport or voices emerge between music passages where recorded. Carry ambience across an image cut or introduce the next environment early when it helps orientation; do not falsely attach a person's voice to another person. Use licensed music, written music or source sound. Shape entry, development and resolution to the picture; avoid a continuous unchanging wallpaper bed. If usable recorded sound is absent, disclose it instead of claiming real sound.

For this Look, a music-only cut does not prove the location-sound promise. Log the usable original-sound moments before assembly and keep at least one meaningful music-to-location-sound passage. Descriptive narration on a stock reel is not location sound. If the available recordings cannot support that passage, flag the source-fit failure rather than calling a music-only substitute a proven Journey sample.

Prefer 9:16 for a short when faces, landmarks and actions survive. Deliberately choose wide if the geography cannot read in a vertical frame, explaining why. Titles can orient the viewer without becoming a slide show. No invented first-person memories, stock voiceover, cloned voices, fake weather or source marks used as branding. Inspect rendered shots and the mix, then review the complete export.

## Evidence and limits

- **Practitioner demonstration, not a controlled result:** Adobe's [travel-music lesson](https://www.adobe.com/learn/premiere-pro/web/add-music-to-travel-videos) demonstrates choosing music to establish a travel sequence's mood. This supports purposeful scoring, not a prescribed genre or promise of retention.
- **Documented editing mechanism:** Adobe's [J/L-cut reference](https://helpx.adobe.com/ca/premiere/desktop/edit-projects/trim-clips/perform-j-cuts-and-l-cuts.html) documents independent sound/picture boundaries. Their narrative use here is an editorial choice.
- **Editorial hypothesis:** arrival/exploration/release and variable pacing are this Look's testable design, not evidence that every trip has a three-act plot.

## Source and delivery limits

Keep the journey and chronology grounded in distinct source moments, not recycled crops or a clip quota. Review the whole moving arc and sound, preserving credits. Public-domain status does not grant endorsement, insignia or likeness rights.
