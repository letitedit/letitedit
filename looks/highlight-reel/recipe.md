# Highlight reel

One event, its atmosphere and its biggest moments: anticipation, activity, peak and release. A highlight is a selected experience, not an arbitrary run of the most spectacular shots.

## Story and coverage

Read the folder's provenance before mixing shots. Identify what event and edition they belong to; do not blend different years or venues into one supposed day. Look for setup, people arriving or gathering, the main activity, reactions where actually recorded, and an aftermath or natural conclusion. If coverage is missing, say so. Never fabricate cheering, pretend an unrelated reaction answers an action, or manufacture a single participant from different people.

A brief peak preview is optional. Establish place and anticipation, build two or three coherent clusters, let the strongest action land, then give an honest end rather than a mid-action stop. Select from the folder in a new order. Mix geography, human scale, working details and action; many similar long-lens views do not replace event coverage. Within a real continuous action preserve its direction and timing, unless an explicitly elliptical montage makes the time jump clear.

## Rhythm, sound and format

The picture may be music-driven, action-driven or alternate both. Use phrase changes and selected accents to build energy; not all cuts need a beat. Let the live event break through where the source supports it. Engines, crowd, announcers or applause have different jobs; identify and audition them, and watch for copyrighted music in an otherwise public-domain recording. Do not substitute generic crowd sound and call it synchronous. Score with licensed or written music when necessary, retain quiet space without silence holes and resolve the last sound. Master and measure the full mix.

Check the encoded opening and every handover from source sound to score; a loud whole-film reading does not prove continuous sound. Keep the readable action, not a fixed hold merely because the music continues. Required credit and non-endorsement wording needs readable lines and contrast over the actual ending, not a tiny legal block that technically exists but cannot be read on a phone.

Default to 9:16 only when the main activity remains legible. Wide formations, team movement or broad performance geography may justify 16:9. Explain that choice. Titles should identify the event accurately, not imply an endorsement or sponsor. Keep logos out of the design language. Finish with actual rendered-frame and audio checks and a complete review.

## Evidence and limits

- **Practitioner guidance:** Adobe's [storytelling lesson](https://www.adobe.com/learn/express/web/video-storytelling) explains purposeful connections between image and sound in short-form stories. It supports intentional selection, not a mandatory conflict arc.
- **Documented mechanism:** Adobe's [J/L-cut reference](https://helpx.adobe.com/ca/premiere/desktop/edit-projects/trim-clips/perform-j-cuts-and-l-cuts.html) supports carrying sound across a picture cut. Whether it improves this event remains a viewing judgment.
- **Editorial hypothesis:** anticipation/activity/peak/release is this Look's coverage test, not a proven universal event formula.

## Source and delivery limits

Choose distinct meaningful highlights, not repeated crops or a clip quota. Check the arc, moving joins and mix across the complete export. Keep source credits and verify incidental rights; public-domain status does not imply endorsement.
