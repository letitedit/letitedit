# One place, one moment

Style: travel/event. A small, truthful visit: recognise the place, watch something happen, leave on its natural release. Pictures and real location sound carry this Look; a host, narrated travel diary and music montage are alternatives, not requirements.

## Choose it when

One coherent place has an observable action with a change and an ending: a clock performance, a market exchange, a ferry docking, a race finish. Several honest angles help but one continuous take can suffice. Do not manufacture an arrival, anticipation or audience reaction. Static beauty shots alone do not prove an event.

## Selection and shape

- Log every clip by picture and sound. Establish location from supplied evidence, not guesses. Record the action's onset, change, peak and release in source seconds, plus what must stay visible. [E1, E2; medium / adapted]
- Open where action is legible, with enough surroundings to orient a stranger. When the source alone cannot identify the place, use a short verified place/event label rather than assuming recognition. It must not claim this is the person's trip or happening today. [E1; medium; label choice: creative / low]
- Preserve revealing action in its real sequence. Remove a genuine wait only if the time jump stays honest; never replay motion as a second event. An uninterrupted take beats decorative cuts. End after the action resolves, not before the payoff. [E2; medium / adapted; E3; source-specific]
- With coverage, change wide/detail/wide for information and feeling, not a timer. Match actual action and geography across angles. Never pass stock from another city or occasion off as this event. [E1; medium; creative safeguard]
- Length follows the event: a compact complete excerpt, not a compulsory cut rate or duration that removes the ending. A slow stretch earns its time when a change is perceptible. [E2; medium / adapted]

## Framing, light and words

Keep the mechanism, participants and surroundings visible throughout. For vertical delivery inspect every moving subject and both edges. If a portrait crop loses the event, contain the wide source in an intentional neutral canvas instead of severing the action. Avoid automatic pan/zoom, synthetic shake and forced transitions. Gentle exposure/white-balance correction is optional; preserve actual time of day. [E1; medium; layout choices: low / creative]

No speech means no invented spoken captions. One factual place/event label can orient, clear of the action and phone controls, with readable contrast and enough reading time. No motivational text or invented host story. Credits go in the supplied description or a discreet end treatment that does not eclipse the event.

For a contained wide picture, unused canvas can hold that orientation without covering either window or participant. Judge the picture at phone size: containment preserves evidence but does not automatically make a small mechanism readable. Keep factual on-screen labels in the visual brief; a checker field defined as words that must be spoken is not evidence for a silent event. Record a visible action/release as the story payoff, never invent a spoken payoff to satisfy a speech-only check.

## Sound

Audition the event: bells, water, applause or footsteps are story, not noise. Keep attacks and decay. Never apply speech-only cleaning to location sound. Repair wind/hiss only where a checked result helps. Music is optional; omit it when source sound is the attraction, otherwise protect the event from masking. Never fake cheers or a different clock's bells. Measure and audition the export, not just stream presence. [E2; medium / adapted; creative safeguards]

## Source and delivery limits

Preserve real event chronology; never reverse moments to invent it. A single event clip is not a multi-place journey or a travel day. Retain creator, source and licence credits and verify audio/incidental rights before publication.
