# Story of the day

An event as an unfolding experience: preparation becomes anticipation, something happens, and the place or people settle afterward. More context and breathing room than Highlight reel, without inventing a presenter or participant.

## Shape from what happened

Read all source provenance and sound, not only the exciting shots. Find an honest time sequence with setup, gathering, main action and aftermath. A weekend collection can tell the event's story, but never label it a literal single day. If no ending or setup is recorded, disclose the missing coverage rather than captioning one into existence. Choose a viewpoint the footage supports: a spectator's impression, the event taking shape or a worker's task. Do not imply one person's personal story without matching coverage.

Select footage from across the folder. Link at least a few small sequences: people preparing a space, activity gaining momentum, an action resolving and space clearing. Use wider geography to orient, human-scale action for participation and close details for texture. Long observational holds are valid when something develops inside them. Do not let slow mean an untrimmed B-roll reel or dialogue stretched by one silence threshold.

## Voice, rhythm and resolution

Use the event's own usable voices where they genuinely explain what is seen; voiceover is not required. Never clone a source voice, invent testimony or turn an unrelated announcement into a false claim. Dates and very short section labels may bridge an honest time jump; avoid wall-to-wall captions on a mostly visual film. Alternate room for location sound with shaped music, rather than a continuous music bed with no dynamics. Sound can anticipate the next space or continue under a detail cut where the relationship is truthful. Check incidental songs separately from government footage rights.

Give the principal action enough time to resolve; let the aftermath earn a closing observation. Use a musical resolution or a recorded sound tail, not a digital-silence hole. Default to short vertical only where the activity survives; wide event geography may justify 16:9. Render and inspect all shots, measure the mix and review the whole video. Pacing, palette, titles and transitions remain editorial options.

## Evidence and limits

- **Practitioner guidance:** Adobe's [storytelling lesson](https://www.adobe.com/learn/express/web/video-storytelling) considers story purpose and image/sound choices rather than requiring conventional conflict in every short. Chronological reconstruction here is our application, not a measured outcome.
- **Documented mechanism:** Adobe's [J/L-cut reference](https://helpx.adobe.com/ca/premiere/desktop/edit-projects/trim-clips/perform-j-cuts-and-l-cuts.html) documents separate sound and picture boundaries. Their use should be motivated by this footage.
- **Editorial hypothesis:** contextual preparation, sustained action and aftermath distinguish this Look from the compressed highlight. No source proves a universal shot length.

## Source and delivery limits

Build the day from real chronology and distinct moments; never fabricate a day from unrelated coverage. Keep source credits, check the complete moving story and sound, and verify incidental rights separately from public-domain status.
