# Performance first

Style: indie short film. The actor's intention changes the picture: a held look, a line landing, an interruption, a choice. Light, pace, framing and cuts serve performance. A film treatment is a separate Theme, not another Look card.

## Choose it when

A scripted scene has coverage of the same people, space and dramatic exchange. Log takes, script variations, eyelines, props and sound first. One angle can carry a truthful performance; don't invent a reverse. Unrelated actors and places must not become a fabricated scene. This is not an explainer, trailer, quote reel or generic moody montage.

## Build the scene

- State the dramatic question and select a take whose intention supports it. Choose delivery and listening, not simply the cleanest transcript. Keep a causal exchange including consequential silence or action. A script is not proof every written word was recorded. [E2, E3; medium / source-specific]
- Make spatial relationships clear before a reaction must carry meaning. A master may orient, or matched looks may do it. Return wide when space or a new arrival matters. Check screen direction, eyelines and props across chosen takes. [E1; medium]
- Cut when attention, intention or information changes. Hold a listener when that tells the story, a speaker when their performance carries it, and a silence when a thought visibly changes. Not alternating faces per line, cutting on a timer or removing every breath. [E1, E2; medium / adapted]
- J/L cuts need continuous speech and believable room tone. Never truncate consonants, mismatch mouth movement or splice an unrelated reaction. Check joins in picture and sound. [E1; medium; safeguards]
- A short is a self-contained dramatic beat: setup, change, landing. It need not keep every exercise line, but must not invent the answer with unrelated reactions. End on a consequence or unanswered choice, not a countdown. [E2, E3; medium / adapted]

## Light and camera centring

Match neighbouring takes gently for exposure and white balance; preserve motivated light direction, skin and shadow detail. Work with existing light, not promised new lamps or recovered clipping. Keep eye room toward the partner, headroom and expressive hands. Symmetry may underline a stalemate; off-centre space may reveal an interruption. Neither is compulsory. [E1, E4; medium; specific centring: creative / low]

For vertical delivery inspect every shot at a 360-pixel-wide phone size, including the master, waiter entrance and final shared look. A portrait crop must not sever the acting pair, expressive hands or eyeline partner. A tiny widescreen strip floating in an otherwise empty black portrait frame is a compromise, not the default. Choose the composition that serves each beat: [E1, E2, E5; medium / adapted; exact layout: creative / low]

- A genuine recorded close-up can fill the tall frame when its take, eyeline, props, mouth movement and room tone join truthfully. Leave eye room toward the partner. A digital crop is a crop, not a newly recorded reverse or reaction.
- A roomier 4:5 or square picture within the tall canvas can enlarge faces while retaining two people or an expressive hand. Inspect the whole motion span, sharpness and new arrivals; use a wider shot when the action needs the lost space. A 720p source is a quality constraint to judge, not an automatic veto on a useful modest crop.
- A contained wide master can establish geography or carry an unbroken truthful exchange. Give the remaining canvas a purpose: a concise neutral scene title and readable dialogue captions, with picture placement and type arranged together. No invented emotional labels, compulsory decorative background, duplicated blurry actors or stacked podcast treatment. Captions may occupy deliberately designed free canvas, but must remain inside the delivery app's visible area rather than its bottom control band.
- Mix these options only when performance and continuity support them. Use a master for spatial information and a genuine close-up for expression, not a cut on every line. Hold the final consequence long enough to read at phone size. If the only honest coverage remains wide, retain it, supply access text, and name the small-face limitation rather than fabricating coverage.

No moving zooms simulating extra cameras or graphics covering actors. Keep the framing reversible. Review the actual export, not just the crop plan; the card poster comes from inside the picture, showing the performers rather than mostly empty bands. [E5, E6; adapted; local practice]

## Film Themes

Use themes: film. Read the chosen Theme and film-treatment-notes.md. Auto may choose clean daylight or observed documentary if the lighting cannot sustain a stylised grade; say which and why. Low-key night, silver noir, golden hour and centred storybook are alternatives only where supported. Respect baked-in shadows: no fake day/night, compulsory grain, automatic teal/orange or opaque overlay over actors. Themes must not change words or invent story. Match shots before consistent restrained treatment. [E4; medium; treatment: creative / low]

## Sound and captions

Production dialogue leads. Audition alternate takes; use matching recorded sound if it improves intelligibility without changing words. Keep quiet room tone across cuts, not a gate to digital silence. Music is optional; silence can strengthen an interruption. No automatic trailer booms, whooshes or fake Foley. Cinestudy's director warns that the sound is not as clean as preferred; AAC presence does not prove usability. Never clone actors or generate ADR. [E3; strong for this source; mix: creative / low]

Dialogue in a muted-feed Short gets access captions by default; omit only when the person asks or the destination supplies an equivalent caption track. Use exact recorded words, calm phrase groups, at most two lines, names intact, no bouncing word-pop or labels telling the viewer how the actor feels. Start around 17 characters per second (20 as a check), 0.8-7 seconds per cue, adapting without dropping words. Judge type at width 360; about 3.4% of the canvas's short side is only a size floor, not a promise of readability. White type on a dark backing should retain at least 4.5:1 contrast against bright noir highlights. [E6; adapted caption guidance, not a universal fiction style]

Lay out picture, title and captions together. Keep one calm caption position in useful free space, away from faces, hands, source text and the target app's buttons; check the target platform zones and caption_fit, then inspect the export at phone size. If a conservative multi-app safe area conflicts with a face, recompose or state the specific delivery limitation; do not silently put text under controls. Inaudible dialogue needs repair, another take or an honest limitation; captions alone don't repair audio. Check the exported mix and complete scene, not just attractive frames.
