# Low-key night

A readable face in a quieter, cooler room. Night is the treatment's mood, not a claim that daylight footage was filmed at night.

- **Light and grade:** keep the person close to the source's exposure and warm-neutral skin; lower room brightness modestly with the live lighting's room setting and cool the room through the inverted face window, so the face keeps its warm-neutral skin. Protect dark hair, eyes and dark clothing. Use broad feathering, not a hard spotlight. A soft vignette may quiet empty corners, kept off the face. Original windows and cast shadows remain.
- **Starting settings (the Theme's picture uses these; adjust to the footage):** lighting `{face_ev: 0.03, face_gamma: 1.12, room_ev: -1.5, window: [2.2, 3.0], feather: 0.8}` (a tall window keeps a cap or hair out of the darkening); the room's cool shift on the same face-following oval, a little wider (1.2 times the lighting's room oval), inverted, as Color Grading that keeps brightness: lift r/g/b -0.01/+0.002/+0.018, gamma 0.94/1.012/1.06, gain 0.88/1.0235/1.12. The darkening comes from the lighting, the coolness from this shift. Check the face after both: skin still reads as skin.
- **Pace and cuts:** let a complete thought land, then cut to the detail that earns the next line. Allow a short beat after a reveal; use a cut-in for information rather than an arbitrary timer. Avoid whooshes or a flash at every cut.
- **Framing:** one clear subject, a little negative space toward the gaze, background highlights checked for distraction. Keep the person's head and gesturing hands; do not automatically centre every shot.
- **Music feel:** sparse low-register texture with a little space; no sustained bass masking speech. A tense or sad score is not mandatory. Respect Music: none and the person's selected source.
- **Captions:** shared neutral boxed captions, not blue low-contrast type. Check the dark room and bright practical lights behind every cue.
- **Optional scene texture:** a very light film-grain pass can add texture to actual scene pixels. Grain is the editor's live Grain filter on the clip. No glow around the person's outline; no false moving light across the face.

**Evidence:** E1 supports contrast and foreground/background separation; E2 supports motivated cut-ins. The particular cool/warm balance and timing are creative choices, not evidence that blue means sadness. Apply shared limits in film-treatment-notes.md.
