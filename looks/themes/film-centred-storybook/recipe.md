# Centred storybook

A frontal, orderly composition with soft pastel surroundings and readable, warm-neutral skin. Deliberate geometry, not a director impersonation.

- **Light and grade:** soften room contrast a little and reduce saturation; let a restrained warm accent coexist with natural skin. Grade the room through the inverted face window, keeping its shapes visible; the window also reaches clothes, so keep the change gentle. Do not recolour the person's clothes or props into invented story facts.
- **Pace and cuts:** let a frontal tableau establish its arrangement, then cut cleanly to the next useful scale or repeated composition. An action can finish before a symmetrical match cut. Do not hold empty space merely to meet a fixed shot-length target.
- **Framing:** centre the primary subject only when the source can support it without cutting off the head, hands or a second person. Align real verticals and use existing geometry. Keep movement inside the frame. If a crop cannot keep the needed subjects, stay wider or use an explicit inset rather than claiming the set was made symmetric.
- **Music feel:** a light, precise instrumental motif with clear phrases; leave room for dry delivery or silence. Quirkiness is optional and should not turn a serious subject into a joke.
- **Captions:** neutral dark-backed type, centred beneath rather than across the face. Keep labels orderly and sparse; no tiny decorative serif text on a phone.
- **Texture:** normally clean. Do not soften the real face or caption edges. No compulsory grain or leaks; a drawn background belongs to the selected Look, not this film treatment.

**Evidence:** E4 supports geometry, frontal views and symmetry as a deliberate film language; E2 supports intentional matching/cut-in choices. Pastels, music and holds are creative starting points. Apply shared limits in film-treatment-notes.md.
