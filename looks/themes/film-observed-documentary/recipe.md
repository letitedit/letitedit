# Observed documentary

The scene still feels witnessed: recognisable colour, real room sound and the camera movement the source actually has.

- **Light and grade:** correct distracting exposure/casts gently; keep the person natural and room detail present. If the live face window is useful, use the smallest separation of these treatments. No dramatic coloured edge, face smoothing or artificial day-to-night.
- **Pace and cuts:** follow what the subject is doing or thinking. Keep the breath that finishes a thought, trim a genuine false start, and allow a reaction or piece of evidence to answer a line. A jump cut may be honest and useful; hide none with unrelated footage that implies a false event.
- **Framing:** preserve meaningful handheld motion already in the footage. Do not add synthetic shake to a static shot or call that authenticity. Stabilise only distracting movement, retaining useful context and hand actions. Use an establishing/re-establishing shot when geography becomes unclear.
- **Music feel:** source sound leads. Music is optional, restrained and separated from speech; do not impose emotional certainty on an ambiguous event. Keep room tone across joins.
- **Captions:** exact wording and names, quiet neutral styling, no rhythmic word flashing over a serious statement. Distinguish quoted speech from added explanation.
- **Texture:** no automatic grain, glow or leaks. Do not imply aged or archival evidence by adding damage.

**Evidence:** E2 supports spatial orientation; E5 is practitioner evidence for subject/emotion-led pacing, not a universal documentary tempo. Neutral colour and minimal split grade are creative starting points. Apply shared limits in film-treatment-notes.md.
