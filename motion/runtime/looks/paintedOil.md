# Oil on canvas: opaque paint with hog-bristle brushes on toned linen, lean block-in to fat impasto, painted dark to light in front of the viewer

A slow, weighty, handmade picture: still life, heritage, a place, a portrait of an object, calm authority, anything that should feel made by a person with a brush. It suits a full painted film, a painting taking shape behind a speaker or a single cutaway. It's the AI's choice for this video.

`const O = M.look.paintedOil.start(c.w, c.h, {scale})` in your block, on one full-frame canvas. `const cv = M.el('canvas', {}, stage); cv.width = c.w; cv.height = c.h; const g = cv.getContext('2d');` gives you that canvas. You write the scene and the subject; this is only the kit.

**How it works.** A picture is a *work*: a surface, a ground and a list of marks in order. Make it once at the top of the block, add every mark there, then draw it each frame: `const pic = O.canvas('still'); ...; return (t) => { O.begin(g); pic.draw(g, {p: M.prog(t, .5, 12)}); };`. `p` 0..1 is how far through its marks the painting is, so it paints itself; leave `p` out for the finished picture. Any p gives the same pixels in any order. Once p is 1 it costs one sprite a frame. `pic.draw(g, {p, x, y, s, r, alpha})`, and `g.translate` and `g.scale` are your camera. `pic.at(n)` says where mark n ends on the 0..1 scale, to land a pass on a word.

**The medium.** Oil is opaque and laid dark to light; every pass covers the last. The mark is a flat bristle stroke: it lands square and full, each bristle lays its own line, the outer bristles run dry first and the end drags into streaks. It carries two colours, what it was loaded with and what it picks up by the end. Ridges stand up at both edges of the brush: the edge turned to the window catches a thin light, the far edge a thin dark. Edges are **found** on the lit side (clipped against what's there) and **lost** in shadow, where the shadow's value meets the wall's so no line is left to find.

**The order that makes it read as oil.** The rub (added by `O.canvas` unless `toned: false`) -> a thin umber drawing -> dark masses with a big brush and lean paint, ground left showing -> mid tones along each form -> lights with a smaller brush and fatter paint -> short thick highlights -> the last dark accents. Big brushes first: a 130 px flat lays a wall in a few steep passes; a small brush does accents only. Strokes follow the form: hoops round a pot, across a pear's section, along a table's grain.

**What it gives**
- `O.canvas(id, {w, h, priming, toned, tone})` the work: linen, priming, weave in the ground and again over the paint.
- `O.strokes({region, step, len, w, dir(x, y), color(x, y), seed, load, dry, impasto, bend, sweep, jit, soft, path})` a pass: strokes over a region (a polygon, `[[x, y], ...]`), each coloured from your light model at both ends. `dir` gives an angle in radians. `color` gives a `#rrggbb` colour, or null to skip that spot; a null at a hard distance leaves a straight cut-off edge, so fade a colour out instead. `impasto` 0 lies flat, 1 stands off the canvas. `soft` feathers it in, so no stroke starts as a square slab. `path(x, y, len, flip)` gives a stroke that follows the form's own curve.
- `O.lay(pic, strokes, {per, clip, out})` puts them on the list. `per` is each stroke's share of the time. `clip` keeps them inside a shape (a found edge); `out: [shape]` keeps them off shapes, so a background never crosses an object.
- `O.drawing(pic, ctrl, {w, alpha, seed})` the searching umber line. `O.rub(pic, tone)` another rub.
- A single stroke by hand: `{ctrl, w, c0, c1, load, dry, impasto, alpha, jit, soft, seed}`. `O.lighten(c, k)`, `O.darken(c, k)`, `O.LIGHT` (points towards the window, from the left and a little above, so a point is lit by how far its offset from the form's centre goes along it), `O.palette`.
- `pic.add(weight, (G, q) => ...)` your own mark, with `G.cur` the surface's context; `pic.pause(weight)` a beat between passes.
- The plain parts: `O.plate`, `O.piece`, `O.smooth`, `O.oval`, `O.mix`, `O.rng`, `O.fractal`, `O.bounds`, `O.inside`, `O.within(G, shape, out, fn)`.

**Shapes and helpers, on every paint and print kit.** Points are `[x, y]`. `O.smooth(pts, closed, per)` a curve through points, `O.oval(cx, cy, rx, ry, n, a0)` a closed loop of `n` points, `O.line(a, b, bow)`, `O.poly(corners, per)`, `O.turn(pts, cx, cy, degrees)`, `O.displace(pts, amp, freq, oct, seed)` a wobble baked into the points, `O.mix(a, b, t)` two `#rrggbb` colours, `O.rng(seed)` a function giving 0..1, `O.fractal(seed, x, y, fx, fy, oct)` smooth noise 0..1, `O.inside(shape, x, y)`, `O.bounds(pts)` `{x0, y0, x1, y1}`, `O.clamp(v, a, b)`. `pic.add` must be called before the first draw.

**What the kit can't do for you.** The light model is yours: `color(x, y)` decides the picture. A painting on `p` is a still that's being made; if it must move after that, paint its parts as separate works with `toned: false, priming` left to you, or drift the camera. A few hundred strokes is plenty; thousands cost time at first draw.

**Cost.** Measured on a scene of about 400 strokes at full size, on a shared PC, headless with a graphics card: median 24 ms a frame while it's made, but 160-280 ms in its busiest moments, when the wide block-in strokes go down, so that stretch can stutter in a live player. No card: 25-27 ms median, and the export takes it in its stride. Finished, it's one sprite. The cost is the number and width of strokes: a few hundred is plenty. The hand's own texture (paper, weave, dots, grain) is a fixed size in pixels, so a frame drawn smaller than the project shows it coarser than the export: check texture at full size.

Refused as everywhere: timers, clock reads, `Math.random` (`O.rng`), the network. Write the name out as `M.look.paintedOil`, or it won't load.
