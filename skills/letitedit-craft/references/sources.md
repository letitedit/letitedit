## Source families and how much to trust them
- **ITE, Inside The Edit** (Paddy Bird, UK broadcast documentary and reality editor since 1995). 54 of the library's 69
  documents. His videos and companion articles repeat each other, so agreement inside ITE is one voice, often shown on
  the same reflective documentary footage. Many demonstrations show before-and-after timings (measured from captions,
  so plus or minus 1-3 s). The "triune brain" explanations in his body-language lessons are teaching metaphors and are
  not encoded here; only the behaviours are.

  check its own. The transcript stops at 36:32.

  themselves. Second-hand; corroboration only. Cited here: `the-4d-editor-techniques-for-showing-the-passage-of-time`,
  `smooth-your-scene-transitions-with-a-sound-bridge`, `how-to-start-a-cut-the-5-edit-drivers`.
- **OURS**: local editing practice; useful direction, not general audience validation.

**Confidence** (as the research defines it): High = two or more independent families agree (for example ITE and an
AOTC editor); Medium = one professional family across several documents or demos; Low = a single mention, a
creator-only claim, a collector's note, or our inference.

## Evidence limits
- "Default to cutting" (Timeline-Bench, outside the library): the library argues against it for long-form (P1, P5).
- Re-engagement beats at 3:00 and 6:00 (a leaked creator memo, outside): replaced by varied gears (P3).
- Interaction-peak and attention-span figures (Kim 2014, Guo 2014; outside): not used.
- Slowing faces to about 80% and keying eyes open after a lie (AOTC pop L302-304): an admitted grey area; excluded
  by D5 for documentary and interview work.
- ITE's limbic and "triune brain" framing (mQT6QnsM2eI 04:41-06:19): behaviours kept, the neuroscience claim dropped.
- Fixed numbers for shot lengths by role, b-roll shares, talking-head caps and jump-cut budgets: mode-dependent and
  mostly untested; they belong in letitedit-modes if anywhere.
