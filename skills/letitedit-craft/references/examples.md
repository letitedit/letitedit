# Worked examples and calibration numbers

Read on demand. The examples show the rules working together on real demonstrations from the sources and on our own
reusable examples. The numbers at the end are calibration points from single demonstrations or anecdotes: use them to sense
scale, never as targets. Rule ids point to SKILL.md.

## 1. The same scene, two destinations (P1, P2, V2)
ITE KAO0n3C0kAg cuts one documentary scene twice with the same structure, about the same b-roll and the same
information, changing only the levers.

| Lever | Kinetic, anxious version (1:25) | Slow, epic version (2:05) |
|---|---|---|
| Music | a dance track, cut to the beat and to single instruments | orchestral score |
| Shots per action | several | one long shot instead of five |
| Action inside the shot | the more frenetic options | calmer options |
| Camera | whip pans, pull focus | locked-off wides |
| Space around lines | speech starts at once, little space | about 10 s of establishing pictures first, wider gaps near the end |
| Line choice | an urgent on-location line added for its tone | the patience line |

The slow cut ran about 1.47x the fast one. Measured detail from the captions: the spacing of list items was similar in
both (about 2-7 s); the extra length came mostly from the establishing opening, fewer and calmer shots and longer
spaces near the end. Lesson for V2: change the levers, keep the structure; the editor also said the film's context
picks between them (don't follow a fast sequence with another fast one: P3). Sources: KAO0n3C0kAg 06:18-16:12,
16:00, 19:52-23:02, 26:29-35:43.

## 2. Messy interview to a scene (D1-D4, B1, P4)
ITE's worked process across three demonstrations (urCAMKTsc6o, xZnHgyeZlsU, 5G2wGolpXxE):
1. **Diagnose the raw answer.** Pauses of about 1 s, 3 s and 1.3 s were flagged as problems in speech meant to be
   fluent; "you know" came 12-13 times in about a minute; one answer had three possible endings (urCAMKTsc6o
   05:41-09:19).
2. **Is there a scene?** Before fixing errors, check the assembly has a start, middle and end (xZnHgyeZlsU 03:40).
3. **Clean up in two passes:** the obvious errors first, then the detail (urCAMKTsc6o 10:07-12:32). Afterwards the
   speaker "sounds definite"; before, the cumulative effect made him sound unsure (07:11, 11:09).
4. **Build sync islands** with about 1 s of space between them so the structure shows (xZnHgyeZlsU 08:16).
5. **Choose b-roll by a visual strategy from what is said:** for a man talking about being alone in the city, mostly
   wides, a few mids, one close-up placed for a reason, two or three reveal pans (xZnHgyeZlsU 11:06-13:52). Decide the
   tone before pulling selects, or the selects reel balloons (5G2wGolpXxE 18:17).
6. **Rough cut:** spread the islands, use tell-then-show (or show-then-tell), bookend with the subject walking toward
   camera at the start and away at the end (5G2wGolpXxE 23:20-25:07; xZnHgyeZlsU 18:57-19:37).
7. **Score:** cut the track to the scene and offer two scored versions with different emotional outcomes
   (xZnHgyeZlsU 24:49-28:35; letitedit-sound).
8. **Re-ask what the scene is about** (xZnHgyeZlsU 33:04).
Scale: a reflective scene grew from about 1:53 dialogue-only to about 3:21 once space and pictures went in (about
1.8x; 5G2wGolpXxE, measured), another from about 1:19 to 2:04 (about 1.6x; xZnHgyeZlsU, measured). Space is
content: a finished talk-led scene is often much longer than its dialogue.

## 3. A cinematic documentary opening, rebuilt (C3, T2, B1)
ITE gwyneGrX9Jw: the first cut was 25 s that told everything with 1-2 s shots ("My name's Dave..." then his life
story). The rebuild:
- no self-introduction; a poetic first line;
- 3-5 s shots instead of 1-2 s;
- sound layered in: waves first, a slow fade-up on the first image, the gull when it enters frame, music later;
- clues dripped from least to most revealing; the confirming line only after the reveal shot registers;
- about 15-20 s of pictures and layered sound before the first word; the key fact (that he lives there) about
  35-40 s in (measured from the captions).
Two professional adjustments: a cold open can be broader and more literal while viewers learn the film's language
(AOTC pop L68-70), and starting from what the subjects love builds the care that later jeopardy needs (AOTC porcelain
L78-84). For a Short, the opposite pressure applies: the premise must read muted at once (JH 2:34; letitedit-shorts),
while the outcome is still withheld (T2).

## 4. Observational footage compressed (X2, B3, C4)
ITE WOX-WdXSOOc cut a 4-minute observational assembly to 1:58 on the first pass: find where it starts getting good
(07:19), keep the good dialogue, then hide the compression with cutaways; cut dead pockets; match the speed and
direction of movement across cuts and enter shots already moving (16:57); accept small time jumps when shots are steady
and the dialogue sounds continuous (24:48); delete back-to-back shots of the same size (46:50); let a new shot register
before the line that reacts to it (47:27); balance empty time (too much feels empty, too little rushed or fake: 56:07).
A one-to-two-minute action may need only about 8 s on screen (ITE zXHKcITf3x8 20:22).

## 5. A Short in the Hoyos shape (P5, S4, T1-T3, C4)
JH's skeleton for her own channel (one creator, her analytics, 2023):
1. a hook that would work as a long video's title and thumbnail, understood muted (2:34);
2. a one-line foreshadow of what the end will show; hook plus foreshadow usually 3 s or less (19:20-20:36);
3. a breather that doesn't stall: she replaced "let's get started" style lines with a line that is itself a story
   beat, and adds no new details after the two key lines (20:52-22:01);
4. a mechanism that shows progress (three things to do, a countdown) (15:23-16:18);
5. but/therefore beats (22:07);
6. pay off exactly what was promised, then twist if the material twists (16:18-17:34);
7. end right after the payoff: trimming one dead final second lifted one video's average retention from 83% to 88%
   (7:07-8:15).
Not frantic by default: in her experience YouTube Shorts rewarded slower, story-led pieces around 34 s, TikTok denser
10-20 s ones (17:38, 29:13). The full Shorts method is letitedit-shorts.

## 6. Repair-story checks
Open on a clear problem, not a greeting or an accidental mid-sentence start (C3, C4). Keep speech coherent
(D4), seed a Plan B before its payoff (S5), and build time cards from real recording dates (S6, X2).
Remove unusably dark or blurry material (S2). A hold must stay alive (P4); show the teardown and the new
part going in when those actions carry the story (B2). Let pictures and music lead where talk adds nothing
(D1). A genuine apparent ending followed by a real setback can meet then twist an expectation (T3);
step-by-step side cards can make progress visible (S4). These are editorial choices, not success guarantees.

## 7. Two revisions, step by step (V1, levers.md)
**"Less flashy"** on a 3-minute vlog. Read the `devices:` note and `letitedit_timeline_read`: 6 whooshes, 9 punch-ins
(mostly 120%), 3 whip transitions, a glitch on the title. Changes: remove 5 whooshes (keep the one on the title move),
keep 3 punch-ins on the biggest lines and step the rest back, replace the whips with straight cuts, remove the glitch;
swap the music for a calmer cue from the same library (`letitedit_soundtrack`); open two gaps after key lines. Check:
device counts fell from 19 to 5; cut rate fell about 10% (`letitedit_measure`); the structure and words are unchanged.
Tell the person: "Took out most of the sound effects and zooms, swapped the music for a calmer track; same story."

**"Give the reveal more breathing room"** at 1:42. Diagnosis from `letitedit_words`: the next line starts 0.3 s after
the reveal line; the reveal shot appears with the line. Changes: the reveal shot now starts 1 s before the line; two
shots before the reveal merged into one longer shot (T4); the face held 1.5 s after the line until the expression
settles (B4); the music's swell moved into the new 2 s gap (letitedit-sound). Mark `hold:reveal`. Check: render
1:30-2:00 with sound (`letitedit_check_range`) and watch it; the gap and the hold are there; nothing else moved.
(These two are illustrations of the method, not recorded cases.)
