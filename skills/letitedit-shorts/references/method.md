# The short-form method in detail

Read on demand from letitedit-shorts. Everything is paraphrased; tags as in SKILL.md and `references/sources.md` (`[GEN]` and `[OURS]` as letitedit-craft defines them). Her numbers are one channel's
analytics (young audience, 2023): defaults, not laws.

## Her skeleton [JH 19:20-22:55, 23:54-25:05]
1. **Hook**: very short, visual, understood without sound, simple enough for a small child, and good enough to be a
   long video's title and thumbnail [JH 2:34].
2. **Foreshadow**: one line on what the viewer will see by the end. Hook and foreshadow together are two lines,
   usually 3 s or less [JH 19:20]. She speaks one in every video, even a plain one: going to the beach, plus a
   promise to surprise someone with $100 at the end [JH 20:14].
3. **Transition**: a breather that keeps momentum. In her banned-menu-item short she replaced "let's get started"
   with a line that moved the story on (she cooked it at home anyway) [JH 21:20]. No new details after the two key
   lines, because viewers forget the first ones [JH 21:45].
4. **Middle**: problem then solution, or but/therefore beats, each one changing the situation [JH 22:07]. Her
   example: a walk told as a chain of "and then" is flat; told with a but (it starts raining) and a therefore (she
   runs home) it holds [JH 22:17].
5. **Payoff, then end** [JH 0:42].

Writing order: the idea, the hook, the last line (with a blank where the real reaction will go), the foreshadow,
then a rough script or bullet points; film; revise; edit [JH 23:54-24:48]. For existing footage: pick the closing
moment and the hook first, then the foreshadow line (said, or as text), then assemble the middle.

## Her examples, paraphrased
- **First frame of a series**: for her one-dollar fast-food remakes she shows the restaurant front with its logo
  centred, the food item, and a short centred price label; the same layout every episode makes the playlist easy to
  binge [JH 10:00-10:48]. She leads with the place, not her face, because more people know the place.
- **Mechanism**: a challenge whose circle keeps shrinking pulls viewers to the end [JH 15:29]. Her easiest version:
  say there are three things to do and show the list, so viewers feel how close the end is [JH 15:51].
- **Expectation, then twist**: a $5 Mother's Day gift. The cold open shows the gift moment; the short follows
  through; then the gift drops and breaks, and a joke lands [JH 16:32-17:34].
- **The last second**: one short's retention graph fell off a cliff in its final second; trimming that second lifted
  average retention from 83% to 88% and the video took off [JH 7:07-7:36].
- **Investment**: a personal, slightly ironic reason (cooking for strangers to pay for her broken kitchen) makes
  viewers care about an ordinary task [JH 1:37-2:14].
- **Plain words**: she checks scripts with a readability tool and aims at 5th grade or below; words like
  "business" or "profit" push the grade up, so she explains them each time instead [JH 3:16, 4:52].

## Hook approaches (write 3-6, offer 3, keep the runner-up) [GEN names; the tests are hers]
| Approach | First frame | First line pattern (invented examples) | Fits |
|---|---|---|---|
| Visual premise | the recognisable thing + 2-4 words of text | "This $12 part fixed a $2,000 problem" | product, repair, travel |
| Stake or goal | the problem, visible or audible | "If this fails, the whole unit comes out" | story, repair |
| Result first | the after, briefly | "Quietest it's ever been. It took two fixes." | before/after, repair |
| Question | the odd thing | "Why does this wheel keep walking off its shaft?" | tutorial, documentary |
| Curiosity | a close detail that still reads muted | "Nobody notices this until it's too late" | documentary, travel |
| Mid-action | the action at its peak | (the sound or the motion is the line) | montage, event, sport |
Every option must pass the same three tests: understood muted, could be a title, said simply. A result-first hook
withholds the how, never the premise.

## Meta phrases to cut from the first 10 s (check 3.2) [GEN list; the rule is JH 21:20]
"let's get started", "let's get into it", "let's dive in", "so today", "today I'm going to", "in this video",
"without further ado", "before we start", "hey guys", "welcome back", "what's up everyone", and any subscribe ask
before the payoff.

## What professional editors add about openings
- Two ways to start [ITE esNXgCJh-yk 08:47-12:10]: **urgent** (time, place, jeopardy and consequence in one
  breath; tight frames, faster cuts, handheld, percussive music) and **poetic** (contemplative; longer, static
  shots; ambient sound). No source tests a poetic start in a swipe feed, so a short defaults to an urgent or visual
  start; use a poetic one only when the brief's feel asks for it and frame 0 still says what this is.
- The 10-second test: play the opening twice, the second time muted, and ask what you feel and whether you would
  keep watching [ITE esNXgCJh-yk 14:08-14:34].
- With no previous scene, the start is the story and every second counts [ITE article how-to-start-a-scene].
- Cold opens are more literal than later scenes: show what is said [AOTC Greatest Night in Pop].
- An opener that lists name, job and history reads like a news report [ITE gwyneGrX9Jw 02:20-03:04].
- Withhold the outcome and the details, not the premise or the stakes [ITE esNXgCJh-yk 05:28; JH 16:47].
- Stakes work in a 60-second short as much as in long-form [ITE D2iBdbNej78 09:08-09:20].

## ...and about endings
- End a section on a short punctuating line or image and cut soon after; when the speaker offers several endings,
  use one [ITE WOX-WdXSOOc 44:52-46:35].
- Music endings: cut on the beat rather than fade [ITE xZnHgyeZlsU 26:40-26:59]; the last phrase must resolve, a
  phrase from mid-track sounds unfinished [ITE xhJLcy8Kzl4 16:16-16:48]; end with the track, on black or a wide
  [ITE KAO0n3C0kAg 21:58-22:10].
- Long-form resolves and adds a forward hook [ITE vUIwB7i-6IQ 05:26-05:46]; a short ends at the payoff [JH 0:42]. A
  pointer to part 2 or the long video is the payoff's last words, not an outro.
- A teaser's shape, from a second-hand note: grab, escalate, change the music's energy, stop the music for the
  final joke [FEP trailer note].

## Conflicts, and how the skill settles them
- **Tight vs breath** (topic-shorts CF-1): cut edge air and meta talk; keep thought-ending breaths and reaction
  beats; get breathing room from story lines, not pauses [JH 20:52; ITE xiJmwycUxTY 07:40].
- **Promise vs withhold** (CF-2): disclose the goal and the shape of the payoff; withhold the outcome and the twist.
- **Is social one speed?** (CF-11): no; pace by feel and platform [JH 29:13 against ITE vUIwB7i-6IQ 04:17].
- **Music under a talking short** (CF-12): no source; keep it under the voice with its peaks in the gaps
  (letitedit-sound).
- **Formula vs freshness** (CF-13): one skeleton per video is fine; vary the mechanism, the twist and the devices
  across videos [ITE 4s7WdcyV1H4 02:52].

## How the mode shapes a short (pointers; the playbooks live in letitedit-modes)
- story / vlog: a personal reason in the first beat; but/therefore; the payoff on screen.
- tutorial: the step list is the mechanism; one idea per beat; plain words.
- interview / podcast excerpt: start on the line; the payoff is a line or a reaction; stay on the face while the
  eyes carry it; captions all the way.
- documentary: the premise still reads muted; withhold the outcome, not the premise.
- product: product, place or price in frame one [JH 10:00]; the first seconds carry the promise [ITE article
  how-to-start-a-scene].
- travel / event: a want, not "our trip" [GEN]; one strong image as the hook.
- montage / transformation: the track sets the cuts; show the after briefly in the hook if the brief allows; end on
  the musical out.
- creative: the brief and its reference set everything; the hook still has to read muted.
