# Documentary mode

Real people and events shaped into a film: observational, interview-led, character-led, historical, or an essay.
Shared craft (purpose line, gears, scenes, dialogue, b-roll purposes, loops, jump-off points, repetition) is in
letitedit-craft; documentary leans on all of it. Tags: `sources.md`.

## 1. What drives it
- **Interviews usually carry the structure and observation the feeling;** some scenes are led by sound or music (a
  countdown built from a sound map, a scene cut to the subjects' own music). Narration only where interviews and
  pictures can't make a link. Check: each scene names its driver; each narration line is marked as a link nothing
  else makes. [AOTC GNIP] [AOTC Porcelain War] [ITE DcA-Jfa2fdw 07:11]
- **Find the story in the footage:** learn the events and the people; string out the interviews by idea (everything
  said on one theme, each cluster labelled); mark what surprises you, since you are the first audience. Expect the
  story, and the opening, to be found late. Check: a story document (bullets, the main person, decision points, the
  end state) exists before the rough cut and is kept up to date. [ITE ZcIVl01h14Y 02:15] [AOTC GNIP]
- **Purpose line:** "This film follows [person] through [decision or change]; by the end the viewer [feels or
  understands] ...". Check: the critic retells the arc from the render. [AOTC GNIP]
- **Truth is part of the brief:** real people's words and events never change meaning (letitedit-craft's boundary,
  applied strictly). Any cheat (audio from another moment under a shot with no visible lips, a reaction moved, a
  place swapped, a face held longer than it was) goes on an approval list in the journal; never slow a face or alter
  someone's eyes automatically. Keep each person's arc true to them, and their safety first. Check: every item on the
  list is approved or removed. [ITE KAO0n3C0kAg 12:01] [AOTC GNIP] [AOTC Porcelain War]

## 2. Structure options
| Shape | Choose when | Avoid when | Tag |
|---|---|---|---|
| A character's arc through their decision points | there is a person to live the story through | they make no choices on camera | [AOTC GNIP] |
| Chronological | events build in order | order alone raises no question | [AOTC GNIP] |
| Emotion first: normal life, then the trouble | caring about the life makes the jeopardy matter | viewers would ask "when is this?" | [AOTC Porcelain War] [AOTC MI] |
| Two intercut streams (a life, and the events it is known for) | a biography | streams that never hand over | [ITE ZcIVl01h14Y 07:14] |
| Observational scenes | fly-on-the-wall footage | scenes with no progression | [ITE WOX-WdXSOOc 00:00] |
| Essay with archive | an argument leads | pictures that only repeat the words | [ITE o-IGEc0sFMg 00:36] |

Check: scene cards (purpose, revelation, end state, tone, gear), a boundary table with the reason to move on at each
change, and a reveal map of what the viewer knows when. [ITE ZcIVl01h14Y 06:14]
- **Care before jeopardy:** show the life, or what the person loves, before the threat. Check: an investment beat
  comes before the first threat. [AOTC Porcelain War]
- **Thread context in,** briefly, where it is needed, so a viewer can come in cold; there is no fixed cap on
  backstory. Check: no exposition block runs over about 20 s without a story event [GEN]; everyone gets context
  before their payoff. [AOTC GNIP]
- **Keep the complications** (exhaustion, a plan failing): a sanitised film feels curated. [AOTC GNIP]
- **Leave out what doesn't pay off,** raises questions the film can't answer, or slows the drive, however good.
  Check: a "what breaks if this goes?" note per scene. [AOTC Porcelain War] [AOTC GNIP]
- **An open ending is allowed** when life is unresolved: an invitation, not a forced bow. Declare it so the loop
  checks accept it. [AOTC Porcelain War]

## 3. Pace profile
- **Documentaries needn't be slow:** archive essays can cut like thrillers, and exciting scenes use fast music, quick
  cuts and crash zooms; reflective scenes sit at gears 1-2. Vary scene to scene; never two of the same gear side by
  side. Check: the gear sequence varies, or a note explains a repeat. [ITE ZcIVl01h14Y 13:10]
  [ITE bCVeyUFGxi8 16:38]
- **Hold after revelations and on faces:** the moment after someone speaks often carries the revelation.
  [AOTC GNIP] [ITE pRpaqx_5jD8 09:41]
- **Observational compression should look like real time:** start where it gets good, cut dead pockets, match
  movement, hide joins with cutaways; when effort or time is the point, show it passing (letitedit-craft).
  [ITE WOX-WdXSOOc 14:23] [FEP 4d]
- **Vary how reveals are treated:** the same shot pattern, music move and sting every time becomes a formula. Check:
  no two consecutive reveals share a treatment. [ITE 4s7WdcyV1H4 06:14]

## 4. Sound
- **Natural sound and actuality first:** build openings in layers (ambience, effects as their sources appear, music
  later), keep on-location lines that back up the interviews, and protect sounds that carry meaning. Check:
  protected ranges are audible; no digital silence. [ITE gwyneGrX9Jw 10:28] [ITE urCAMKTsc6o 13:33]
- **Music is chosen per scene,** never wall to wall; switch it off when sections start to feel the same. Check: a
  music coverage map; dry scenes tagged as intentional. [AOTC GNIP] [AOTC Invite]
- **Silence once:** build toward it; no two silent scenes near each other. [AOTC Porcelain War] [AOTC Invite]

## 5. Text and graphics
- **Name every person at first appearance;** dates and places as short cards where the viewer needs them; a dated
  countdown when there is a real deadline. Check: each speaker is named at first appearance. [AOTC GNIP]
- **Archive, photos or a graphic fill context gaps;** set up a premise or threat graphic early when later scenes rely
  on it; animate only when it adds meaning nothing else can. [AOTC GNIP] [AOTC MI] [AOTC Porcelain War]
- **Subtitle** other languages and hard-to-hear speech (letitedit-captions). [AOTC Porcelain War]

## 6. Shot choice and b-roll
- **Literal early, subtext later:** pictures that show what is said in the cold open; later, lines over contrasting
  images let the viewer lean in. [AOTC GNIP] [AOTC Porcelain War]
- **Loose "wallpaper" shots only on neutral stretches;** emotional words get behaviour (a stare, a breath, hands).
  [ITE 9jRZIqPQHm8 05:37] [ITE jN3TIBIQlac 04:43]
- **Interviews as performance:** the face for feeling, b-roll for information, each section's last line on camera
  (interview-podcast). [AOTC GNIP]
- **Self-shot footage shows what matters** to the people who filmed it. [AOTC Porcelain War]
- **No visible lips under borrowed audio,** and each such cover goes on the cheat list. [ITE WOX-WdXSOOc 30:14]

## 7. Openings and endings
- **Choose the opening template:** urgent (time, place and stakes in one breath; tight, quick) or contemplative (a
  reflective first line, 3-5 s shots, sound layered in, clues from least to most revealing, the confirming line only
  after the reveal shot registers). Withhold outcomes and details, never the premise or a threat later scenes depend
  on; a cold open can be more literal while viewers learn the film's language. Cut the beginning last. Not: "Hi, my
  name is..." or a stack of facts. Check: facts stated in the first 30 s against the template; the muted 10-second
  test. [ITE esNXgCJh-yk 05:28] [ITE gwyneGrX9Jw 02:20] [AOTC MI] [AOTC GNIP]
- **End on a short line or an image that lands:** the last speaker on camera, the music resolving, or an open
  invitation; a chapter break lands on a win, not a downer. [ITE WOX-WdXSOOc 46:28] [AOTC Porcelain War] [AOTC MI]

## 9. Common failures and how to check
| Failure | Check | Tag |
|---|---|---|
| A fact-dump opening | facts in the first 30 s against the chosen template | [ITE gwyneGrX9Jw 02:20] |
| A scene about two things, or almost-right material | a one-line purpose per scene; the critic lists lines that could go | [ITE FOJhAl9IyPg 07:57] |
| Neighbouring scenes in the same gear | the gear sequence | [ITE bCVeyUFGxi8 16:38] |
| Every reveal treated alike | reveal treatments logged and varied | [ITE 4s7WdcyV1H4 06:14] |
| Wall-to-wall music | the music coverage map | [AOTC GNIP] |
| An unnamed speaker | a name at each first appearance | [AOTC GNIP] |
| An interview sentence built from several answers | the sentence-to-answer map; the cheat list approved | [ITE KAO0n3C0kAg 12:09] |
| A question raised and never answered | the loop ledger, unless the ending is declared open | [AOTC Porcelain War] |
| Non-linear order that confuses | the critic never asks "when is this?" | [AOTC MI] |
| A sanitised film | at least one real complication kept when the footage has one | [AOTC GNIP] |

## 10. Evidence strength
The strongest of the modes. Most of the library is a documentary editor's craft (Inside The Edit), backed by two
documentary Art of the Cut interviews (Porcelain War, The Greatest Night in Pop) and the feature editors. Caveats:
Inside The Edit is one voice across dozens of documents; its numbers (3-5 s opening shots, about 10-20 s of picture
before the first line, a 4-minute assembly halved) are single demonstrations; the cheat list and the ban on automatic
face retiming are our synthesis, since no library source states an ethics policy; consent, releases and minors are
not covered.
