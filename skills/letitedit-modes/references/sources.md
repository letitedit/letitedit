## Outside the library ([YT], [RES])
- [YT] YouTube Help, "Video chapters": the first timestamp at 0:00, at least three timestamps, each chapter at least
  10 s (tutorial). Check the current page or `letitedit_platforms` before relying on it.
- [YT] YouTube Help, "Add end screens to videos" (support.google.com/youtube/answer/6388789): end screens run in the
  last 5-20 s of a video at least 25 s long, up to four elements on a 16:9 video (interview).
- [RES] EBU R128: -23 LUFS integrated, true peak <= -1 dBTP, for broadcast deliverables (all modes).
- [RES] Apple Podcasts audio requirements: -16 LKFS integrated (+-1 dB), true peak <= -1 dBFS (interview).
- [RES] W3C WCAG 2.x success criterion 2.3.1 (three flashes or below threshold); broadcasters apply similar limits,
  e.g. ITU-R BT.1702 (creative).
- [RES] Mayer, Multimedia Learning (2nd ed., 2009): segmenting (one idea at a time), signalling (cues that point at
  the essential element) and coherence; Moreno and Mayer (2000), Journal of Educational Psychology 92(1): background
  music added to a narrated lesson reduced learning (tutorial). Named from general knowledge, not re-read for this
  skill: supporting evidence, not a letitedit result.
- [RES fox-tree-1995] Fox Tree, J. E. (1995), "The effects of false starts and repetitions on the processing of
  subsequent words in spontaneous speech", Journal of Memory and Language 34, 709-738: words after a false start were
  recognised more slowly than the same words with it removed (mainly mid-sentence or after a discourse marker); words
  after a repetition were not slowed, and in two experiments were faster (interview). From the abstract; the full
  text was not read: one lab study, word monitoring, not viewer liking.
- [GEN] web and social -14 LUFS is letitedit's existing delivery setting; the earlier verification found no official
  YouTube, TikTok or Instagram upload target (audit FI10). The reference band of 0.8-1.25x: the 0.8x floor came from
  Timeline-Bench via the old letitedit-fine (FN10); the ceiling is ours.

## Podcasts and multicam ([WEB], [REF])
- [REF podcasts] Six popular two-person YouTube podcasts, plain to heavily produced, measured on frame sheets (one
  frame every 5 s over the first 2.5 min and a 75 s stretch from the middle). Typical shot: about 5-6 s on a casual
  couple's show (a wide and a single of each, cut on the talk and the reactions); about 12 s on a raw long-form show
  that cuts to whoever speaks; about 25 s on a cinematic studio show; one show held a side-by-side for long stretches;
  a busy branded show cut about 9 times a minute. None burned captions into the conversation itself.
- [REF podcast-ending] The last 4 minutes of one couple's hour-long video podcast, watched and measured: the talk
  closes on a laugh at a callback in the wide; one host thanks the viewers and says what comes next in her single
  (about 7 s); a hard cut to a silent end screen (a cream panel with a follow line, a dark panel left for the
  platform's links), held about 12 s. One show: thin.
- [WEB descript-multicam] Descript help, "Automatic multicam" (help.descript.com/visuals/automatic-multicam): one
  camera per speaker's audio; a "show only active speaker" style; cutaways in longer monologues every ~10 s
  (frequent) or ~30 s (occasional), or none.
- [WEB bh-podcast] B&H Explora, "A Guide for Editing Your Video Podcast": stay mostly on whoever speaks; cut to the
  others for reactions to add energy.
- [WEB obsbot-setup] OBSBOT, "Multi-camera podcast setup", and [WEB obsbot-workflow] "Multi-camera podcast production
  workflow": sync every camera and mic first (waveform, timecode or a clap); the wide covers crosstalk and
  interruptions and hides edits; hold an angle through short "mm-hm"s rather than switching on each.
- [WEB finchley-cutting] Finchley, "Video engineering in a professional podcast post-production: when to cut and
  why": silent reaction shots placed over awkward joins in the conversation.
- [WEB descript-crosstalk] Descript blog, "How to edit crosstalk in video": keep natural overlaps; they carry the
  feel of the conversation.
- [WEB autoedit-review] AutoEdit, "AI camera cuts for a multicamera podcast": automatic switching is a draft; the
  editor decides where a reaction, the wide or a held shot serves the moment better.
- [WEB sixcolors-overediting] Six Colors on podcast editing: with too much editing, speech starts to sound artificial.
- [WEB descript-fillers] Descript, "Podcast editing basics": a few filler words sound natural; remove the distracting
  clusters (a tool maker's guide).
- [WEB audition-stutter] Adobe Audition community thread, "How do you edit the stuttering in your audio": editors cut
  the longer stutters because they make what the guest means hard to follow (one practitioner).

## Local practice [OURS]
- Load only the mode playbooks relevant to the brief, together with the required stage skills.
- Design explanatory graphics for this video's idea rather than filling a generic template.
- Ground a setback and its cause in footage; never invent a failure to make a story.
