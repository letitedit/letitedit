# Interview / podcast excerpt mode

People talking with someone: a sit-down interview, a podcast or livestream, a conversation, a panel, cut down into a
piece or into excerpts and clips. The words and the faces are the material. Shared craft (pulling, cutting down,
re-arranging and retiming dialogue, reactions, the honesty boundary) is in letitedit-craft. Tags: `sources.md`.

## 1. What drives it
- **Dialogue drives; find with text, choose with ears and eyes.** `letitedit_words` and `letitedit_search` find what was
  said; `letitedit_speakers` labels who talks when (name each voice once and the words carry the names);
  `letitedit_sync` lines up cameras and separate microphones by their sound. Choose by delivery, energy and face, not
  by the text alone. Check: each kept line has a delivery note, not only its words. [ITE xiJmwycUxTY 09:05]
  [AOTC Porcelain War]
- **Work in stages:** pull everything on the subject, cut down, re-arrange into story order, retime, then add cover
  and music (letitedit-craft). A fluent, compelling speaker can run nearly intact. Check: the dialogue stops changing
  before cover goes on. [ITE iAKUVUD7fG0 04:27] [AOTC GNIP]
- **Purpose line:** "In this clip [speaker] [argues, reveals or tells] [one thing]; the viewer leaves [knowing or
  feeling] it." One thought per excerpt. Check: the critic restates the one thought. [ITE FOJhAl9IyPg 02:49]

## 2. Structure options
| Shape | Choose when | Avoid when | Tag |

| One answer, cut down | a single strong thought | it needs context it lacks | [ITE iAKUVUD7fG0 07:19] |
| Question, answer, follow-up | the answer depends on its question | the answer restates the question | [GEN] |

| One theme across speakers or answers | several voices on one subject | a list of opinions with no turn | [ITE iAKUVUD7fG0 04:57] |
| Two views set against each other | a real disagreement | a disagreement made by cutting | [GEN] |
| Highlights of a long episode, a card per excerpt | promoting an episode | excerpts that need the episode | [GEN] |

Check: the island list gives each island one job and its link to the next; space between islands (picture, music, a
breath) lets a key line land. [ITE xZnHgyeZlsU 07:49] [AOTC GNIP]

### Excerpts: first and last lines
- **The excerpt stands alone:** no dangling "it", "that" or "he", no "like I said", no start mid-list. Fix it with the
  speaker's own earlier setup line, the host's question, or a short context label; never with words from elsewhere
  that shift the meaning. Check: a cold reader of the transcript can say what it is about. [JH 2:34] [GEN]
- **The first line:** a question the clip answers, a premise, a strong or doubtful claim, or an emotional line. Check:
  its type is logged. [ITE iAKUVUD7fG0 11:12] [ITE D2iBdbNej78 07:11]
- **The last line:** a short punctuation line, or the line where the voice resolves (often the revelation); one
  ending even if the speaker offered three. Check: listened to, the clip ends on a finished, falling sentence; there
  is no second ending. [ITE urCAMKTsc6o 08:30] [ITE iAKUVUD7fG0 08:49]
- **The meaning stays the speaker's:** never join parts of different answers into a new sentence, drop a negation or
  a condition, or put an answer after a different question; `letitedit_find_takes` flags takes that differ in numbers,
  names or negation, so never swap between them. Guests are third parties: no exceptions without the person's
  approval. Check: every rendered sentence maps to one answer; the removed words hold no "not", "never", "unless" or
  "but"; the critic compares the clip with the full answer. [ITE KAO0n3C0kAg 12:09] [GEN]

## 3. Pace profile
- **Mostly gears 1-3; a heated debate or quick banter can run 4.** Retime moment by moment, keep breaths at thought
  ends, hold on the face after the key line. Not: one pause threshold for the whole recording. Check: a held beat
  after each key line; no machine-gun stretch. [ITE DcA-Jfa2fdw 04:35] [ITE pRpaqx_5jD8 09:41]
- **Short excerpts are tighter, not breathless:** trim the air before the hook line and after the payoff; keep the
  breath that ends a thought and the beat a laugh needs. [JH 20:52] [ITE xiJmwycUxTY 07:40]
- **Visible jump cuts** suit a creator or entertainment audience; in a calm or premium piece, cover them or cut
  between angles. Check: the jump-cut count fits the brief's tone. [ITE A2RYo1xNSA4 08:03]

## 4. Sound
- **The voices are the mix:** speakers levelled to match, room tone in every gap, no digital silence
  (letitedit-sound). Check: no speaker jumps in loudness at a cut (`letitedit_check_audio`). [ITE DcA-Jfa2fdw 09:00]
- **Keep what carries meaning:** laughs, sighs, the pause before a hard answer. [ITE WOX-WdXSOOc 28:56] [AOTC GNIP]
- **Music sparingly:** none under continuous talk, or a low bed without melody; music between excerpts and under
  cards. [AOTC Invite] [ITE 2aux2Y9-4m8 03:51]

## 5. Text and graphics
- **Name every speaker at first appearance** (the name and why they matter, as a lower third or a caption label).
  Check: each speaker's first appearance carries the name within a couple of seconds. [AOTC GNIP]
- **Question cards** when the viewer must read the question; captions on all speech in vertical clips
  (letitedit-captions; `letitedit_caption_fit` keeps them off faces); a pull quote of the key line only in the exact
  words. Check: quoted text matches the transcript word for word. [JH 29:50] [GEN]

## 6. Shot choice and b-roll
- **Interviews are performances:** stay on the face when the eyes or the voice carry the line; cover truncated,
  informational bites; each section's last line on camera. Not: hiding a speaker for a whole piece, which moved
  viewers less. Check: the on-camera share per speaker is reported; every section ends on camera. [AOTC GNIP]
- **B-roll makes space and hides joins;** never visible lips under audio from another moment.
  [ITE xZnHgyeZlsU 16:35] [ITE WOX-WdXSOOc 30:14]
- **Punch-ins** on a single camera at a change of thought, not on every sentence. [ITE 4s7WdcyV1H4 05:32] [GEN]

### Speakers
- **Who to show:** the speaker while their words carry the feeling; the listener when the reaction means something
  (doubt, a laugh, surprise), cut a few frames to half a second after its trigger, never to tell people how to feel.
  Check: every reaction cut has a stated purpose. [ITE D2iBdbNej78 04:49] [ITE JZXMZNhL2Zw 02:56]
- **Several cameras:** cut to a new speaker at or just after their first word; hold a wide two-shot for fast
  back-and-forth and crosstalk; match the cameras' look (`letitedit_match_shots`). Check: the critic never loses who is
  talking. [GEN]
- **Overlaps:** keep an overlap that carries energy (an interruption, shared laughter) on a wider shot; never borrow
  a word from another moment to smooth it. [AOTC Invite] [GEN]

### A whole conversation on several cameras (multicam)
- **Sync, then one cut:** line the angles up with `letitedit_sync`; run `letitedit_speakers` (count: the number of
  people) on the angle with the clearest sound; look at one frame of each angle and name who it shows; then
  `{op: multicam}` cuts the whole stretch as one change. Not: hundreds of single inserts. Check: the reply's shot
  count and time per angle look like the reference. [WEB obsbot-workflow] [OURS]
- **Which voice is whose:** tell the speaker ids apart by listening (`letitedit_hear` on a few of each id's words) or
  by voice pitch, then put each id in the right angle's `shows`. Not: by which mic hears an id louder, which says
  little about who is who and can swap the two, so every "speaking" shot shows the listener. Check: on a stretch of
  the cut, the person on screen in a "speaking" shot is the voice heard (listen, or compare pitch), not just the
  shot's own label. [OURS]
- **One sound:** one source plays all the way (`sound: best` measures which); only the picture switches. Not: each
  camera's own sound at each cut, which jumps in level and room tone. Check: `letitedit_check_audio` shows no level
  step at angle changes. [WEB descript-multicam] [GEN]
- **Who to show:** the speaker's single while one person talks; hold through short "mm-hm"s and one-word answers
  rather than chasing them (`min_shot_s`); the wide for crosstalk, quick back-and-forth, shared laughter and the
  pause before a new topic; a reaction of the listener now and then inside a long turn. Check: the critic never
  loses who is talking. [WEB bh-podcast] [WEB obsbot-workflow] [WEB obsbot-setup] [AOTC Invite] [GEN]
- **Pace from the reference, not the defaults:** set `min_shot_s` and `max_shot_s` from the Look or the reference's
  measured shots. Popular two-person shows run from about 5-6 s a shot (a casual couple's show) through about 12 s
  (raw long-form) to about 25 s (cinematic studio); automatic switchers offer a cutaway every ~10 or ~30 s in a
  monologue. Check: the reply's median shot is near the reference's. [REF podcasts] [WEB descript-multicam]
- **Hide jump cuts by changing angle:** where the talk itself is cut down, an angle change at the cut hides the jump
  (`hide_jump_cuts`); a silent reaction over an awkward join does too, as long as no lips move under sound from
  another moment. [WEB finchley-cutting] [ITE xZnHgyeZlsU 16:35] [ITE A2RYo1xNSA4 08:03]
- **An angle that starts late or stops early** is used only while it recorded; after it, that person is on the wide
  or another angle that shows them. Say so in the hand-off. [OURS]
- **Treat the switching as a draft:** watch a few minutes at low resolution (`letitedit_check_range`), then `pins`
  the moments where a reaction, the wide or a held shot serves better. Tighten, cut down and reorder on the main track
  as for any talk: the angles follow it. [WEB autoedit-review] [OURS]
- **Keep natural overlaps** (they carry the conversation), on the wide; captions of a long conversation usually go
  to YouTube as a file rather than burned in. [WEB descript-crosstalk] [REF podcasts]

### Restarts and doubled words in a conversation
- **Cut the false starts, keep the repetitions.** Cut a sentence begun, dropped mid-way and begun again ("we're pretty
  sure he... we're pretty sure he dragged it off"), and a stumble that blurs what's meant. Leave doubled words ("the
  the", "and and"), a phrase said twice whole ("rat traps, rat traps"), one person echoing the other, and a quick
  self-correction that adds words ("not a mouse, not a tiny little mouse"): listeners process words after a repetition
  no slower (in one study, faster), while a false start mid-sentence slows them; and scrubbing every slip makes a
  conversation sound scripted. Not: a stammer cut on every doubled word, which also breaks the picture of a
  continuous talk. Check: the fine stage and the review count only the false starts (they read the conversation from
  the brief's mode, the Look's `talk`, or a multicam cut); the rest show as one minor line. [RES fox-tree-1995]
  [WEB sixcolors-overediting] [WEB descript-fillers] [WEB audition-stutter] [GEN]
- **How clean the talk is can be set:** a brief's `talk: clean` asks for every restart cut (a tight highlights
  clip), `talk: natural` keeps a conversation's texture in any kind of video; a Look learned from a reference may carry
  `talk` from what the reference keeps. [OURS]

## 7. Openings and endings
- **Open on the line,** or on a short picture with a caption that frames it; no "welcome to the show". Check: muted,
  the first frame and its caption say what the clip is about. [JH 2:34] [JH 21:20]
- **End on the last line, held a beat on the face** (long), or right after the payoff word (Short); a highlights reel
  may end on one card pointing to the full episode. [AOTC GNIP] [JH 0:42] [GEN]
- **A whole episode or a long conversation ends on a closing beat:** a line that wraps the talk up, answers the
  opening question or lands a callback, then the hosts' own thanks and goodbye when they gave one, kept whole ("see
  you guys later, bye" is part of it), a beat held on the face or the wide, then the cut or an end screen. With no
  closing line in the footage, make one honestly: a summing-up line moved to the end (meaning unchanged), a short
  outro over a held shot, or an end card. Not: stopping mid-exchange, or just before the source's own goodbye. A
  YouTube end screen sits in the last 5-20 s (videos of 25 s or more). Check: the last line ends a sentence and a
  thought; the review flags a last line the source runs on from and a goodbye left just after the cut.
  [ITE urCAMKTsc6o 08:30] [REF podcast-ending] [YT] [GEN]

Loudness: a podcast (audio, or video for podcast apps) -16 LUFS, true peak <= -1 dBTP [RES]; clips for the web -14
LUFS [GEN]; broadcast -23 LUFS [RES]. `expected_words_audible` stays on. Critic questions: does each excerpt make
sense cold; does it mean what the full answer meant; is it always clear who is speaking; does any join sound like two
thoughts glued together; does a long cut end on a closing beat rather than stop mid-conversation?

## 9. Common failures and how to check
| Failure | Check | Tag |
|---|---|---|

| Meaning changed at a join | the sentence-to-answer map; removed negations | [ITE KAO0n3C0kAg 12:09] |
| Breaths and pauses amputated | held beats after key lines; breaths at thought ends | [ITE xiJmwycUxTY 07:40] |

## 10. Evidence strength
- **Strong:** shaping dialogue (stages, sync islands, first and last lines, retiming, breaths) and interviews as
  performance: Inside The Edit across several demonstrations, backed by The Greatest Night in Pop's editors.
- **Medium:** reactions and listener shots (Inside The Edit and several Art of the Cut editors).
- **Thin, outside the library:** multicam conversations and podcast pace: practitioner and tool-maker web guides that
  agree with each other [WEB], and shot lengths measured on six two-person podcasts [REF podcasts]. Crosstalk and
  clipping long episodes are otherwise [GEN]. Keeping repetitions and cutting false starts rests on one lab study
  of spontaneous speech [RES fox-tree-1995] and practitioner guides that agree [WEB]: medium. Podcast loudness is an external platform spec [RES]. The excerpt-honesty rules are our synthesis of the
  sources' test: does the change alter the information or the meaning?
- **Thin:** how a whole episode closes rests on one show's measured ending [REF podcast-ending] and general practice
  [GEN]; the end-screen window is platform documentation [YT].
